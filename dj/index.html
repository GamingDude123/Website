Autopilot DJ drop in tracks — it plays the set

Each track is analysed in your browser: tempo and beat grid, the first beat of every bar, key (as a Camelot code), and where its intro, breakdowns, drops and outro are. Then:

  1. Order. The next track is chosen for harmonic fit, tempo distance and where the energy should be going.
  2. Choose a move. Bass swap: the new intro rides over the old outro with its low end cut, and the basslines trade places on the bar where its drop lands. Drop swap: the old breakdown builds (filter closing, riser, loop roll, echo throw) and the new drop lands on the one. Echo out: when tempos are too far apart to match.
  3. Lock it. Both decks follow the same tempo curve, so bar lines stay aligned while tempo glides to the incoming track’s own.
  4. Schedule it. Every EQ move, sweep and swap is placed on the audio clock ahead of time, so a busy page cannot make a swap late.

This is signal processing and rules, not a neural network or a language model, and nothing is uploaded. It copies the techniques of club DJing; it has no taste, crowd or ears. Analysis is an estimate — if a key or downbeat is wrong, fix it on the track row. Tempo changes are pitch-coupled, like vinyl.
